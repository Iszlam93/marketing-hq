# Research 2b: Buying signals

**The question:** which companies show the problem **right now**?

A buying signal is a public, dated sign that a company has the problem today: they are hiring someone to fix it, they raised money to grow, a rule is coming into force, or they just changed something that creates the need. Job ads are usually the best signal: they are dated, written by the buyer's own company, and say what the new person must do.

## Steps
1. **Name the trigger in one sentence:** "A company that is `<hiring for X / raising money / subject to rule Y / switching Z>` has `<problem>` now." The trigger decides what you search for. Never search for your own product's name.
2. **Fix the searches before you look.** Write 3 to 5 short searches in the buyer's words (the job title or the event, not your product category). Fix the region, the time window (90 days is a good default) and the sites. Write them down exactly, so anyone can rerun them. Do not change them after seeing results.
3. **Collect to a fixed depth.** For example, the first 5 pages of each search. Record for each result: search used, company, title, link, date.
   - With a **prospecting connector** (for example Apollo.io job postings, ZoomInfo or Crunchbase signals), run the same searches there and record the filters used. Ask before spending credits.
   - Without one, use public job boards, company careers pages and news. Read only: no applying, saving, following or messaging.
4. **Count in steps**, and write the count after each:
   `all results → truly the trigger (drop recruiters, duplicates, unrelated roles) → in the target industry → right company size`
   Show each step as "out of how many" and a percentage.
5. **Read at least 10 in full.** Note what the company asks the person to do, and the exact words used for the problem (quotes under 15 words, with link). Count how many mention each problem theme.
6. **Compare groups.** If the data allows, put the candidate groups side by side: how many signals each has, and how often each names the problem.
7. **Write the pattern** in 2 or 3 sentences, with how sure you are.

## What to write in the evidence log
```
Trigger: <one sentence>
Searches (fixed before looking): <exact searches, region, window, sites or connector filters, date run>
Funnel: <A all → B truly the trigger → C in industry → D right size, with % and "out of">
Themes in ads read in full (N read): <theme: count of N>
Words buyers use: "<quote>" (<link>)
By group: <group: signals, theme counts>
Pattern: <2 to 3 sentences, Shows / Assumes>
```

## Checks
- [ ] The searches are written down exactly and were not changed after seeing results.
- [ ] Every funnel step has a count and "out of how many".
- [ ] At least 10 items were read in full, or all of them if fewer.
- [ ] Every quote is under 15 words and has a link.
- [ ] Nobody was contacted; no logged-in-only data was used.

## Example (fictional)
Trigger: "A trade firm hiring a dispatcher or scheduling coordinator has outgrown the whiteboard and phone calls." Three searches, UK, 90 days. 600 ads → 140 truly a dispatch or scheduling role (23%) → 55 at trade firms (39% of 140) → 30 at firms of 5 to 50 staff (55% of 55). Of 15 ads read in full, 9 mention "missed or late call-outs" and 6 mention "winter peak".
