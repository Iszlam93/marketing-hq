# Research 2c: Buyer voice

**The question:** what do buyers say about the problem, in their own words?

The best messages reuse the words buyers already use. This file collects them from public places where people talk about the problem, and counts how loud each pain really is. It does not replace talking to customers; it shows what people say in public, which is only the people who post.

## Steps
1. **Write the question in one line:** "What do `<buyer role>` say about `<problem>`?" List 3 to 6 themes you will look for before you read (for example: cost surprise, time wasted, missed work, trust, switching pain). You may add themes while reading; mark them as added.
2. **Choose the places.** Where does this buyer talk? For example Reddit, Hacker News, industry forums, review sites, community groups, conference talks. Public pages only. No accounts, no posting, no voting, no messages.
3. **Fix the searches.** Write 4 to 8 searches in the buyer's words, not yours. Record each search, place, date and sort order. Read the top results by relevance and by most recent.
4. **Pick 10 to 20 threads.** Prefer threads where the speaker is the buyer (owner, manager, lead) over end users or onlookers. Skip promotions and vendor posts. Record how many you looked at and how many you kept.
5. **Take notes per thread:** link, date, size (replies or votes), who is speaking (if stated), 1 to 3 short quotes (under 15 words, word for word), and the theme.
6. **Count honestly.** Report "N of M threads" for each theme, never "customers say". A theme in fewer than 3 threads is weak.
7. **Separate the voices:** buyers (decide and pay), users (do the work), onlookers. Do not mix their counts. A loud group may be the audience to leave out, not the buyer.
8. **List the objections:** what people say against this kind of product. These become the questions your message must answer first.
9. **Write the pattern:** the loudest pain, the words to reuse, and what this sample cannot show.

## What to write in the evidence log
```
Question: <one line>   Places and searches: <list, date>   Threads: <looked at> → <kept>
| Theme | Threads (N of M) | Who is speaking | Strength | Example quote (link) |
Words to reuse: "<phrase>" (<link>): <the problem it names>
Objections: <objection: N threads>
Pattern: <2 to 3 sentences, Shows / Assumes>
```

## Checks
- [ ] Searches, places and dates are recorded; looked-at vs kept counts are given.
- [ ] 10 or more threads, or the shortfall is stated.
- [ ] Every quote is word for word, under 15 words, with a link.
- [ ] Counts are "N of M threads"; buyers, users and onlookers are counted apart.
- [ ] Themes in fewer than 3 threads are marked weak.
- [ ] No usernames are collected beyond what a link shows; nobody was contacted.

## Example (fictional)
In 16 threads from trade business owners, "losing a job because nobody answered the phone" appeared in 7 of 16, and "the whiteboard falls apart in winter" in 5 of 16. Engineers in the field complained about too many apps (4 of 16), a user voice, not the buyer's. Objection: "my team won't use another app" (6 of 16).
