# Research 2d: Competitors

**The question:** what do competitors claim, and what true claim does nobody make?

The useful finding is rarely "who is best". It is the claim that is true for your product and that nobody else is making. This file also produces the list of claims you should not make.

## Steps
1. **Pick 5 to 10 competitors** across three kinds: direct (same job), nearby (same buyer, different job) and the alternative (do it by hand, build it yourself, do nothing). One line on why each is in. Always include what the buyer does today without any product.
2. **Fill the same card for each**, from their live public pages, with the date:
   - headline and sub-headline (short quote, with link)
   - their three main claims
   - who the page speaks to
   - how it treats each problem theme from 2b and 2c: headline, mentioned, or absent
   - price, as listed
   - proof offered: customer stories, logos, numbers

   With a **web extraction connector** (for example Firecrawl), read the pages in bulk. With a **search and web data connector** (for example Semrush or Similarweb), add traffic and top search terms. Otherwise read the key pages by hand.
3. **Check the pages are current.** Note redirects, rebrands and acquisitions. If you only saw a summary or a search snippet, say so.
4. **Check their ads.** Search public ad libraries (for example LinkedIn's or Meta's) for each competitor and for key phrases. Record what they promise and what none of them says. Not finding something only counts for that library and date.
5. **Build the grid:** competitors down, problem themes across, each cell headline / mentioned / absent.
6. **Find the open ground:**
   - **Crowded:** many competitors, same words.
   - **Owned:** one competitor, strong proof.
   - **Open:** nobody says it, and your product can prove it.

   An open claim with no proof from your product is a **hypothesis**, not a message.
7. **Write the "do not say" list:** claims a competitor owns, and claims your product cannot prove yet.
8. **For each main competitor:** where they win, where you win, the objection a buyer will raise, and an honest answer. Never state a weakness without a source.

## What to write in the evidence log
```
Competitors: <name: kind: why included>
Grid: | Theme | <Comp 1> | <Comp 2> | ... |   (headline / mentioned / absent, page and date)
Ads: <what they promise, what nobody says, library and date>
Crowded: <...>   Owned: <...>   Open: <..., proof from product or "hypothesis">
Do not say: <claim: reason>
Pattern: <2 to 3 sentences, Shows / Assumes>
```

## Checks
- [ ] Every competitor has a card with links and dates.
- [ ] "Do it by hand" or "do nothing" is included as an alternative.
- [ ] Rebrands, redirects and acquisitions are checked.
- [ ] The open claim is backed by what the product does today, or marked hypothesis.
- [ ] No competitor weakness is stated without a source.
- [ ] A "do not say" list exists.

## Example (fictional)
Seven scheduling tools all lead with "save time on admin" (crowded). One owns "built for large fleets" with named customers. Nobody talks about winter peaks or missed call-outs (open), and the product can show jobs reaching the engineer's phone in one tap. Do not say: "fewer missed jobs", until a customer can confirm it.
