## Post
Sprinklr opened a beta of six marketing AI agents last week, and the one I'd look at first is the compliance agent.

The six cover ideation, creative, campaign execution, compliance, community and budget. Sprinklr says people keep the approvals, and their product chief calls the design "human in the middle".

The compliance agent checks content against brand standards, internal policies, regulatory and channel rules, and flags exceptions for a person before launch. That's a good job for an agent. But it can only check against what someone has written down.

One customer example in the coverage stuck with me: a consulting firm with about 15 versions of its logo in use. An agent can flag that. It can't decide which version is the right one. Someone has to own that decision before any agent is useful.

So my view is that the boring work comes first. In Marketing HQ, every product file has a list of approved claims, each with its source, and a short list of claims to avoid. The content agent can't use a claim that isn't on the list. Writing those lists took longer than building the agent.

It's early for Sprinklr too. No test results are published yet, and the release doesn't say exactly where the humans step in. I'd want to see that before I'd call it human in the middle.

If you could hand just one of those six jobs to an agent tomorrow, which one would it be?
