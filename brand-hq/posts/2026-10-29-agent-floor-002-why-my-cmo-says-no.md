## Why
Shows the product marketing thinking inside the build (positioning, source of truth, governance). This is what PMM hiring managers care about.

## Post
The most useful thing my AI CMO does is refuse.

In Marketing HQ, the CMO agent runs a small team: a foundations lead for positioning and ICP, a materials lead for battlecards and decks, and a go-to-market lead for launches and campaigns.

Early on I asked it to change our positioning after a lost deal. It didn't. It asked for evidence first: a pattern across win/loss interviews, or a release that changes who we sell to.

I built that in on purpose. If positioning moves every time someone loses a deal, nobody downstream trusts it, and agents make that problem faster. Without one approved source of truth, every agent writes its own version of the message, and they all sound confident.

So the foundations are versioned. Each battlecard and deck records the version it was built on, and when positioning changes, only the affected material gets flagged.

The slides show how it decides.

It's still a prototype on sample data, but it has already changed how I think about briefing people, not just agents.
