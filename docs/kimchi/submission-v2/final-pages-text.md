### The three messages I test (same LinkedIn ad, image and offer; only the headline changes, so any difference comes from the message)

A · Control (my bet)Autonomous agents for your whole team. Limits set before anyone starts.Tests the need for control.

B · PaceSkip the build. Start the agent rollout.Tests the need for speed over building in-house.

C · RoutingFrontier models where quality matters. Lower-cost models for the rest.Tests routing, the reason Kimchi's only customer quote gives.

### Who sees them

Cell 1 · finance, hiring now11 firmsThe finance firms from my job-ad search. Each gets 1:1 outreach: a one-pager built from its own job ad, a LinkedIn note and 3 emails (LinkedIn only in Germany).

Cell 2 · finance look-alikes~300 firmsSimilar UK and EU finance firms, up to 5,000 staff. They see ads A, B and C, then get 3 emails (LinkedIn only in Germany). This is where the three messages compete.

Cell 3 · tech, the side clue~300 firmsTech firms, same job titles. They see ad A only, with no outreach. If tech responds better than finance, I rethink the segment.

## CIf the test works: turning it into an AI process

This first round was hand-built. To scale it without adding headcount, I'd turn each step into a trigger, a skill and an automation, with a person reviewing anything that goes to a buyer.

 | Trigger | Skill (what the agent does) | Automation (what happens next)

 | A new AI-rollout job ad | Reads the ad, sorts it by the same categories (governance, spend, build or buy, pace) and scores the fit | Creates the account in the CRM and alerts the seller

 | A high-fit account | Drafts a custom one-pager from the firm's own job ad, like the Raisin example | Goes to the seller for review before anything is sent

 | A lead form or call | Tags the build-or-buy answer and any objection | A weekly summary of what buyers say, which feeds the message

 | Monthly competitor check | Reads competitors' sites and LinkedIn ads, and flags changes in message | Updates the battlecards

 | Weekly results | Compares ads, calls and pilots with the rules set before launch | Alerts me when a success or stop rule is crossed

Guardrails: public or licensed data only (LinkedIn's terms don't allow scraping, so job data would come from a licensed provider or company career pages); a person approves every message to a named buyer.

## DWhat I'd change in how Kimchi goes to market

What I found that the brief didn't ask for, but the team should see.

 | What I found | Why it matters | What I'd do

 | The proof doesn't match the headline | The homepage leads with governance: "a single platform… with consistent security, governance and cost controls at every step". But its only customer quote, from a Sr. Director of Engineering next to the Akamai logo, praises the Auto router: it "keeps the quality of a single frontier model" while using cheaper models where they're enough. LinkedIn and the launch press release lead with "2.5x cheaper". | Use the router as the proof under the control message: control over which model does which work, at frontier quality. Get one customer story about limits from the pilot

 | "Before, not after" is unclaimed | DX, Jellyfish and Devin all talk about AI spend after it happens. No one, Kimchi included, says the limit is set before the work runs. | Make it the line Kimchi owns on the site, in ads and in sales calls

 | The real competitor is "build it" | About half the AI-platform hires are told to build it themselves, often on free LiteLLM. Claude Code is a tool Kimchi includes, not the rival. | Sell to the person hired to build: "switched on, not built"; a comparison page vs building on LiteLLM

 | Gaps that would cost deals | No cap per single run; no published case study; no cloud marketplace listing, while Devin and Kilo have both; and a free plan that needs your own model key, while Devin gives credits and Kilo gives models. | Per-run cap and smart budgets to product; first case study from the pilot; AWS Marketplace listing

 | Risks a buyer will raise | The default models are Chinese-origin (GLM, MiniMax, Kimi), and that isn't stated up front. Certificates are held at Cast AI Group level, not by Kimchi alone. | Say both openly in a one-page security pack; let buyers approve or block models; consider an EU-origin model option
