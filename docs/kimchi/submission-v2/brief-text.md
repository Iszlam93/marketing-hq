# The brief (text extracted from the PDF)

Sr. PMM Practical Exercise: Kimchi Context Kimchi is Cast AI's newest product: an AI platform for engineering organizations. It provides the infrastructure to run autonomous agents, manage and govern AI usage, control costs, and understand the value their AI investment is generating. Our goal is to help engineering teams move from using AI as an individual productivity tool to operating AI agents reliably and at scale across the organization. Kimchi enables developers to delegate coding tasks to autonomous agents that run remotely. A developer can describe a task, let the agent work independently, respond if the agent needs input, and review the resulting code or pull request. Kimchi is new, and the market around it is moving fast. We have early users and strong curiosity signals, but no validated ideal customer profile and no proven story yet. That means we could tell many different stories to many different audiences, but we can't tell all of them at once. As a senior PMM on Kimchi, the most important call you will help make is this: who do we go after first, what do we say to them, and how would we find out fast if we're wrong? This exercise tests your ability to make that call with public information, turn it into something a seller or a landing page could actually use, and design a cheap, credible way to validate it. What to deliver (max. 2 pages) 1. The bet (about half a page). The segment you would target first and the message you would lead with. Write it as something we could actually put in front of that audience: a positioning statement plus a primary message a seller or a page could carry. Not a description of what your positioning would contain. 2. The evidence (about half a page). What publicly available signal supports this bet, what is missing, and how confident you actually are. Cite your sources. Distinguish clearly between what the evidence shows and what you are assuming. Include what you deliberately excluded: segments, claims, or channels you chose not to pursue and why. 3. The test (about one page). One validation experiment you would run next to learn whether the bet is right or wrong. This should be a spec someone could execute without asking you follow-up questions: channel, 2-3 messaging angles you would test, targeting, budget, timeline, and, critically, success criteria and kill criteria. Explain what decision you would make in each outcome, including an inconclusive one. Tips ● Work from public sources only: competitor sites and docs, reviews of adjacent tools, community discussions (Reddit, HN, Slack/Discord), job postings, ad libraries. We care about what you can find, not what you already know. ● Use Kimchi yourself if you can. It is not required, but it counts. ● Every bet requires cutting something. Tell us what you deliberately left out, and what would make you put it back in. ● You may use any AI tooling. Be ready to explain how and where you used it, and which decisions were yours. ● There is no "correct" segment or story. We are interested in your reasoning and how it holds up, not whether it matches what we would have done. ● Logistics ● Expect up to 4 hours total. Please stop there: we are testing judgment, not endurance. ● Note your actual time spent when you submit. ● You'll present to the Kimchi team, including product and engineering leadership, for about 60 minutes, with most of the time spent on follow-up questions and discussion rather than a re-read of the document. ● Presentation format is entirely up to you. We're not scoring slides or polish. ● Your work will not be used commercially. If you have questions, email me.
