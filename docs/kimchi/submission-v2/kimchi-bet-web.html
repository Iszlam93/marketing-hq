Positioning

For platform leaders at UK and EU financial firms scaling coding agents, Kimchi is the platform where agents work on their own inside limits set first: budgets per team, developer and agent, the right model per task, in our cloud or yours. Unlike Claude Code or Cursor, which limit only their own tool, it covers every agent run through it, and it's switched on, not built.

What the seller says

Your new hire gets limits, routing and cost per PR on day one, in our cloud or yours. They start on the rollout, not the plumbing.

5The test: 7 weeks, about $7k

Main question 1Which message makes platform leaders act: control, speed or value?
Main question 2Will they buy a platform, or build their own?
Main question 3Does finance or tech respond better, and to which message? Both see all three ads.

The three messages I test (same LinkedIn ad, image and offer; only the headline changes, so any difference comes from the message)

A · Control (my bet)Autonomous agents for your whole team. Limits set before anyone starts.Tests the need for control.
B · SpeedChange one URL. Your team's Claude Code gets limits and routing.No migration: developers keep their tools. Tests onboarding speed.
C · ValueMore merged PRs per AI dollar.The right model per task, and the cost of every merged PR. Tests value, which tech asks for most (71%).

Who sees them

Cell 1 · finance, hiring now11 firmsThe finance firms from my job-ad search. Each gets 1:1 outreach: a one-pager built from its own job ad, a LinkedIn note and 3 emails (LinkedIn only in Germany).
Cell 2 · finance look-alikes~300 firmsSimilar UK and EU finance firms, up to 5,000 staff. They see ads A, B and C, then 3 emails (LinkedIn only in Germany). The three messages compete here.
Cell 3 · tech look-alikes~300 firmsTech firms, same titles, up to 5,000 staff. The same ads A, B and C and emails, so finance and tech compare like for like.

CIf the test works: turning it into an AI process

This first round was hand-built. To scale it without adding headcount, I'd turn each step into a trigger, a skill and an automation, with a person reviewing anything that goes to a buyer.

TriggerSkill (what the agent does)Automation (what happens next)
A new AI-rollout job adReads the ad, sorts it by the same categories (governance, spend, build or buy, pace) and scores the fitCreates the account in the CRM and alerts the seller
A high-fit accountDrafts a custom one-pager from the firm's own job ad, like the Raisin exampleGoes to the seller for review before anything is sent
A lead form or callTags the build-or-buy answer and any objectionA weekly summary of what buyers say, which feeds the message
Monthly competitor checkReads competitors' sites and LinkedIn ads, and flags changes in messageUpdates the battlecards
Weekly resultsCompares ads, calls and pilots with the rules set before launchAlerts me when a success or stop rule is crossed

Guardrails: public or licensed data only (LinkedIn's terms don't allow scraping, so job data would come from a licensed provider or company career pages); a person approves every message to a named buyer.

DWhat I'd change in how Kimchi goes to market

What I found that the brief didn't ask for, but the team should see.

What I foundWhy it mattersWhat I'd do
The proof doesn't match the headlineThe homepage leads with governance: "a single platform… with consistent security, governance and cost controls at every step". But its only customer quote, from a Sr. Director of Engineering next to the Akamai logo, praises the Auto router: it "keeps the quality of a single frontier model" while using cheaper models where they're enough. LinkedIn and the launch press release lead with "2.5x cheaper".Use the router as the proof under the control message: control over which model does which work, at frontier quality. Get one customer story about limits from the pilot
"Before, not after" is unclaimedDX, Jellyfish and Devin all talk about AI spend after it happens. No one, Kimchi included, says the limit is set before the work runs.Make it the line Kimchi owns on the site, in ads and in sales calls
The real competitor is "build it"About half the AI-platform hires are told to build it themselves, often on free LiteLLM. Claude Code is a tool Kimchi includes, not the rival.Sell to the person hired to build: "switched on, not built"; a comparison page vs building on LiteLLM
Gaps that would cost dealsNo cap per single run; no published case study; no cloud marketplace listing, while Devin and Kilo have both; and a free plan that needs your own model key, while Devin gives credits and Kilo gives models.Per-run cap and smart budgets to product; first case study from the pilot; AWS Marketplace listing
Risks a buyer will raiseThe default models are Chinese-origin (GLM, MiniMax, Kimi), and that isn't stated up front. Certificates are held at Cast AI Group level, not by Kimchi alone.Say both openly in a one-page security pack; let buyers approve or block models; consider an EU-origin model option
