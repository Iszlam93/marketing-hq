## 2. Pricing and motion
- Free tier (Shows): open-source option, downloadable, self-run, bring your own inference; "Community tier. No credit card required." - https://kimchi.dev/pricing
- Entry price (Shows): Coder $20/mo ($25 credits, max 3 seats). Teams $35/seat/mo ($45 credits, max 150 seats, Studio, budget monitoring, RBAC). Enterprise custom (volume discounts, VPC self-host, 24x7 success manager). Code Review add-on $15/user/mo. - https://kimchi.dev/pricing
- Motion (Shows): both. Self-serve sign-up and install script, plus "Book a demo"/"Book a call" for Teams and Enterprise. Early-adopter page offered a $250 bonus and $50/mo credits (as indexed). - https://kimchi.dev/earlyadopters

- Assumes: product-led bottom-up funnel (free/open source, cheap seats) feeding a sales-led enterprise upsell off Cast AI's existing customer base.

## 5. Who the GTM is aimed at
- Shows: the site speaks to four buyers at once: platform leads (deployment), security (data boundary, certs), finance (hard caps, attribution), developers (keep your tools, free start). The enterprise page is security-and-finance heavy.
- Assumes: primary buyer is the platform/engineering lead, with security and finance as veto and co-sign roles; developers are the adoption entry point (free tier, Discord, KubeCon offers, YC credits).
- Consistency (Assumes): fairly consistent on "governed" and cost control across site, LinkedIn tagline, and blog. One drift: the GA press release and LinkedIn About lead with "2.5x cheaper", while the homepage leads with governance; the blog also pushes benchmarks (a developer/quality message). Garrison's review shows a perception gap: dev-side value is routing and open models, while enterprise claims rest on governance.
- Gaps (Assumes): no case studies, no comparison pages, no analyst proof, no marketplace listing found.
