### Summary: ad-library themes
- Cost and AI spend is already an advertised theme, but mostly by the measurement vendors and by one agent vendor: DX (token spend per developer), Jellyfish ("Is your AI spend paying off?"), Cognition ("What if your AI budget went to the engineers who use it best?"), Cursor ("cut AI spend by about 40%"), Tabnine ("value of your AI investments").
- Governance is advertised by Kong (LLM policies, Forrester Wave). Portkey advertises product launches and an analyst mention, not governance in the snippets seen.
- Productivity, legacy refactor and agent scale-up (GitHub, Cursor, Tabnine, Cognition) is the most common theme overall.
- Finance and regulated buyers: only Cognition names banks (Citi, Itaú) in ad text. No advertiser's ad text that was seen mentions UK or EU regulation, FCA, DORA, auditability or model-risk. Cursor's ads are personalised enterprise outreach but did not name finance in the snippets seen (PayPal and Coinbase appear as proof points).
- Nobody was seen saying: per-agent or per-team budgets enforced before spend happens, cost per merged PR as the headline metric for autonomous agents, or a UK/EU regulated-firm angle for an AI platform team. Absence is based on truncated ad text, so treat as indicative.
- Formats: gated ebooks and reports (GitHub, Tabnine, Jellyfish, DX), event registrations (Jellyfish), analyst badges (Kong, Portkey), and executive-profile posts boosted as ads (Portkey, Jellyfish). Cursor runs outreach-style sponsored messages.

### Summary: top 3 recurring buyer complaints
1. Unpredictable cost. Copilot (premium requests vs per-token), Cursor (moving pricing, seat billing, per-user pools), Devin (consumption billing) all draw cost-surprise comments. Links to Kimchi's spend budgets: budgets set before work runs, not a bill afterwards.
2. Budget and admin controls are weak or paywalled. LiteLLM complaints are about key budgets and enterprise-only features; Cursor's per-user limits and pooled usage sit in Enterprise. Links to governance: per-team and per-agent limits, audit logs, and access controls as a standard feature.
3. Hard to see or prove what the spend bought. Seen in vendor-authored sources (Harness, Jellyfish, DX, Tabnine ads) more than in direct reviewer quotes. Links to cost-per-PR reporting. Treat as strong in advertiser messaging, weak in primary review evidence.
