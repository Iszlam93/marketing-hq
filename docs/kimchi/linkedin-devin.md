## What it means for Kimchi

1. **Devin is moving into exactly our segment.** A London office, the UK team up 200% in six months, and a former JPMorgan architect hired to work with financial services leaders. Competition for UK finance will be direct. The bet holds (the segment is real enough for Devin to invest in), but the "why Kimchi" must be sharper.
2. **Devin shipped a version of "smart budgets" on 19 Oct.** Limits rise automatically for engineers who use it well, up to an admin ceiling. That's close to the product idea in appendix D, so the ask becomes competitive parity, not a nice-to-have.
3. **Devin argues against blanket caps.** "A blanket usage cap is a hiring freeze in disguise." Kimchi's "limits set before anyone starts" can be read as a blanket cap if it's worded badly. Our wording must stress limits **per team, developer and agent**, not one company cap.
4. **Proof drives engagement.** The Citi post (14,000 engineers, 30% vendor spend cut) got the most reactions by far. Named customer numbers beat product posts.
5. **Their content mix:** customer proof, security, its own benchmark, product launches, London events, hiring. Free credits come up again and again as the entry offer.

## Still true
None of these posts says limits are enforced before the work runs. Devin's spend message is about *allocating* budget to the best users, an efficiency story, not limits set first.
