## 2. Pricing and motion

Source for all of this: https://kilo.ai/pricing, unless stated.
- Free tier: $0 platform; open-source VS Code, JetBrains and CLI extensions; Auto Free models, BYOK or local models at $0. Shows.
- Teams: $15 per user per month; 14-day Enterprise trial on new accounts. Shows.
- Enterprise: custom, quote-based; adds SSO/OIDC/SCIM, audit logs, SLA, model/provider restrictions, private gateway. Shows.
- Gateway: pay-as-you-go "exact provider rates", no markup; 5% fee on credit purchases. Shows. (5% fee also on the Cursor comparison page.)
- Kilo Pass: $19 / $49 / $199 per month with bonus credits. Shows.
- Cloud compute: per second; Code Review $0.33/hr, Cloud Agent $0.60 to $1.20/hr. Shows.
- Motion: hybrid. Self-serve and PLG at the bottom (free install, no card), trial then demo then sales at Enterprise, plus a "Speed Run" embedded-engineer onboarding. Shows (https://kilo.ai/enterprise).
- Open source: MIT, GitHub Kilo-Org/kilocode, about 27.8k stars, 3.3k forks. Shows. https://github.com/Kilo-Org/kilocode
- How they make money: seat fees (Teams, Enterprise), the 5% credit fee, Kilo Pass bonus-credit subscriptions, cloud agent compute, and Enterprise contracts priced separately from inference. Shows for the line items. That the 5% fee and Pass are the main margin on inference is Assumes.

## 5. Who the GTM is aimed at

- Two-layer motion. Shows: homepage and pricing target the individual developer (free, no card, install) and open-source community (MIT, Product Hunt, Reddit, HN). The enterprise page targets platform, security and engineering leaders plus finance (SSO, SOC 2, spend transparency, sales-led).
- The loudest push now is an Enterprise land-grab from Cursor (banner, campaign page, comparison page, blog, LinkedIn all carry it). Shows.
- Core wedge across both layers is model freedom plus zero markup (no lock-in, transparent spend). Shows. Assumes the finance angle is meant to counter per-seat or bundled-credit pricing from IDE vendors.
- Consistency: high on message ("open, any model, at cost"), lower on numbers (1M / 3M / 5M users). Assumes the gap reflects different definitions (installs vs "Kilo Coders") or stale copy.
- Gap relevant to Kimchi (Assumes): Kilo's proof is community scale and a few small customers; no named enterprise logos, no testimonials, no analyst coverage, so enterprise credibility rests on SOC 2 and the Anaconda parent.
