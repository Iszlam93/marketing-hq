# Audit of `PLAN.md`

**Verdict:** The finance segment is a defensible first bet, but the plan presents a directional sample as a validated ICP and asks a roughly $5,000 test to make distinctions it is unlikely to measure. The largest panel risks are unverified product promises and decision rules that do not match the planned sample.

## 2. Claims versus evidence

- **Product control is overstated.** “Verified” hard caps at `PLAN.md:57–65, 175` means a limit-setting screen was seen; no spend was run to test enforcement (`product-walkthrough.md:3, 9`). The walkthrough confirms reporting for **Claude Code, Kimchi and OpenCode**, not policy and budgets over “every coding agent” (`PLAN.md:65, 216`; `product-walkthrough.md:11`). “In your own cloud” remains a vendor claim; the walkthrough did not verify deployment (`PLAN.md:61, 65`).

- **Cost per merged PR is visible in the interface, not proven end to end.** The free plan ran no task, and PR reporting may require each developer to enable it locally. The plan’s primary line is a reasonable **message to test**, but “see what every merged PR costs” implies completeness that has not been shown (`PLAN.md:21, 59, 67, 178, 217`; `product-walkthrough.md:3, 12, 20, 27–29`). The plan correctly avoids claiming ROI at `PLAN.md:178`, but “proving value” elsewhere still risks equating an activity cost with business value.

- **Compliance language outruns verification.** “DORA-ready vendor,” “designed around DORA,” and “coding agents your regulator can live with” imply a regulatory and procurement conclusion. The walkthrough did not establish SIEM export, centrally enforced PR reporting, controlled CLI versions, or deployment and data-location details (`PLAN.md:51, 65, 218, 303–306`; `product-walkthrough.md:13, 20–21`). The evidence file describes DORA obligations through contracts and explicitly says there is no blanket EU residency rule (`EVIDENCE.md:43–47`). The plan also groups UK and EU firms under one regulatory message without showing that DORA applies to each target.

- **The competitor “gap” is narrower than its wording.** “Nobody combines control with proving value” and “Nobody speaks to an AI platform lead at a finance firm” are observations from pages scanned, not market-wide facts (`PLAN.md:166–167`; `competitor-messaging.md:199–207`). The scan itself calls Devin a nearby control/outcome competitor, lists spend and governance claims from Copilot and Cursor, and notes finance material from GitHub and Kong (`competitor-messaging.md:88–105, 130–140, 203–207`). Its inputs were summarised page fetches, with some relevant pages unread (`competitor-messaging.md:3–5, 211–219`). “Portkey (now Palo Alto)” at `PLAN.md:164` also reads as a completed ownership change when the scan could not confirm closing (`competitor-messaging.md:7–10, 217`).

- **The ICP rationale is stronger for a test than for a lock.** The jobs establish visible hiring, not tool adoption, budget, purchase intent or a preference to buy (`PLAN.md:26, 32–35, 124`; `finance-size-check.md:171–174`). “Giant banks build their own or buy direct” is an unsupported generalisation at `PLAN.md:32, 40`; the source mainly shows their size and hiring. “Finance is the only one [of finance and tech] with strict data and vendor rules” at `PLAN.md:30` is too categorical for the evidence (`JOB-POSTINGS-ANALYSIS.md:62–67`). Among the 11 small-account ads, only **three** are unequivocally developer or engineering facing (`tier1-accounts.md:503`), and four of the seven platform-role classifications are the issue the test must probe, not proof of a coding-agent purchase.

## 3. Logic and measurement

- **Exclusions need consistent return rules.** Individual developers have one (`PLAN.md:39`); large finance firms have a vague reference condition (`:40`); tech’s “responds as well” lacks a metric and sample rule (`:41`). Healthcare and the Middle East have **no condition for re-entry** (`:42–43`). The 90-day source has **seven healthcare companies**, which supports deprioritising them, not dismissing their need (`finance-size-check.md:40–47`). “No Kimchi signal found” for the Middle East means **not researched**, as the one-month method says (`JOB-POSTINGS-ANALYSIS.md:5–10`).

- **The proposed comparisons do not consistently change one variable.** A1–A3 change angle, but also change the concrete promise and regulatory framing; each needs the same audience, format, offer, timing and budget to read as a message comparison (`PLAN.md:191, 208–221`). A1 versus A4 can compare industry only if company size, geography, titles, creative and exposure are matched; A4’s targeting is not specified (`:210, 214–221`). Tier 1 versus Tier 2 changes hiring signal **and** 1:1 outreach, personalisation, account selection and ads, so it cannot isolate timing (`:186–210, 223–231`). Track 0 is a different warm-account motion and should not enter those comparisons (`:207`).

- **The success rules cannot support the promised decisions.** A 25% CTR lead is below the planned detectable lift; no minimum impressions or confidence rule is attached to finance versus tech (`PLAN.md:245, 263–264`; `test-benchmarks.md:92–98, 157`). “Responds clearly better” and “no difference” have no defined numerator, denominator or decision boundary (`PLAN.md:266`). Three of seven engaged accounts means **42.9%**, but one account changes that rate by **14.3 percentage points**; two meetings is a useful sales signal, not a validated segment (`:265, 278, 313`). “Engage” is undefined, and zero replies is incompatible with a success measure that may count clicks or other engagement. The $500 account-ad allocation also assumes seven named companies can form a deliverable matched audience; the source gives a **300-member minimum per ad set** and warns that EEA matching may limit reach (`test-benchmarks.md:52–54`; `PLAN.md:246, 312`).

- **The decision tree skips plausible mixed results.** “Finance wins, no clear message → change the offer” does not follow: it might mean the angles are equally good or underpowered (`PLAN.md:273`). “Tech wins” does not distinguish a narrow CTR lead from qualified meetings (`:274`). “Inconclusive → two more weeks” adds no sample or budget requirement, then defaults to control regardless of evidence (`:276`). The kill rule permits stopping at 5,000 impressions per angle, while the stated design needs 15,400 per angle for its larger CTR difference (`:245, 263`). The opening calls this a **five-week** test, but the dated schedule is two weeks of validation, four weeks live and one readout week: about **seven weeks** (`:5, 9–16, 251–257`).

## 4. Fit to the brief

The plan supplies one named segment, positioning, a primary message, public signals, citations through its working files, angles, budget, timeline and an inconclusive outcome. Its weakest brief requirements are **confidence stated next to each decisive inference**, a clear **shows versus assumes** distinction in the presentation itself, re-entry conditions for **every exclusion**, and measurable decisions at the budgeted sample. The product walkthrough is first-hand use of a free account rather than a public source (`PLAN.md:5, 171`; `product-walkthrough.md:1–3`); label it separately from the public evidence. The source list at `PLAN.md:317–321` is too broad for a panel to trace the decisive numbers and claims quickly.

For a two-page submission, cut the process diary and most channel inventory (`PLAN.md:69–85, 280–296`), the eleven-row account table in favour of the few accounts driving the test (`:127–157`), and future scale ideas such as the report, roundtable and next DORA test (`:233–239, 309`). Keep their underlying evidence available for panel questions. The plan itself acknowledges work beyond the four-hour guide (`:315`).

## 6. Top five fixes, ranked

1. **Make claims panel-safe:** separate interface observations, untested enforcement, and vendor assertions; remove or qualify “every agent,” “in your own cloud,” “DORA-ready,” and regulator-facing copy until Product verifies them (`PLAN.md:51, 57–65, 216–218`; `product-walkthrough.md:3, 9–13, 20–21`).
2. **Reduce the test to decisions this budget can support:** choose a primary comparison, specify matched audiences and exposure, and make the other tracks directional (`PLAN.md:182–221, 241–278`; `test-benchmarks.md:92–98`).
3. **Replace vague thresholds with denominators and outcomes:** define engagement, qualified meeting, minimum impressions and a genuine inconclusive branch; treat 3/7 as a learning signal (`PLAN.md:259–278`).
4. **Present the ICP as a confidence-rated bet:** show what the hiring data measures, the developer-facing and regulated-account gaps, and a concrete return condition for each excluded segment (`PLAN.md:26–43, 120–125`; `tier1-accounts.md:503–515`).
5. **Correct the audit trail and compress it for two pages:** fix the 36-duplicate label and 15/16 denominator, distinguish the five- from seven-week timelines, and put direct citations beside the few numbers and claims that drive the decision (`PLAN.md:5, 95–118, 251–257, 317–321`; `finance-size-check.md:29–34, 161–167`).
