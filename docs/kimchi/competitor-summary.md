## Where Kimchi can't win, so it shouldn't try

Claude Code and Codex belong to the companies that make the models. Kimchi can't beat them on:
1. **Model quality** ("the best coding AI"). Their models win the public tests.
2. **The individual developer** ("make each developer faster"). That's their home ground, and Kimchi's individual users already left.
3. **Price for one person.** They subsidise usage (an HN comment: "the subsidies from big labs can eat your business").
4. **Brand trust and proof.** Gartner Leaders, bank rollouts.

Devin owns "**an AI engineer that delivers outcomes**", with bank proof and a $10M guarantee. Kilo owns "**any model, free, community**".

## So Kimchi should avoid
- Saying "better than Claude", or comparing model quality.
- Selling to individual developers or talking about personal productivity.
- Leading with price or "cheaper".
- Calling itself an "AI engineer" (Devin's words).
- Saying "open models" or "any model" as the headline (Kilo's ground, and buyers read "open" negatively).

## And claim what none of them do
- **Sell to the organisation, not the developer:** the platform or engineering leader who must roll agents out to everyone.
- **Limits set before the work runs,** across teams, developers and agents. The big tools limit only themselves, and rivals report spend afterwards.
- **Don't replace Claude, include it:** teams keep Claude for the hard work, on their own key, inside Kimchi's limits.
- **Your cloud, when security asks.**

**One line for the panel:** *"I don't position Kimchi against Claude. Claude is a model and a tool. Kimchi is where a company runs its agents, Claude included, under limits it sets in advance."*
